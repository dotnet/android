Architecture

Initialize

The service loads the host, starts CoreCLR, and prepares the MAUI route corpus.

Why binding matters: a one-shot broadcast left CoreCLR as a cached process that Android reclaimed quickly. A live service connection ties its importance to the foreground shell and gives Android a rebind path after failure.

The packaging trap

The build injected NativeAOT into the CoreCLR process.

Declaring any secondary Android process caused the SDK to generate another NativeAOT runtime ContentProvider for it—even though the Activity and service were Java-only.

Without intervention, PID B would initialize both NativeAOT and CoreCLR, recreating the signal and exception-handler problem we were trying to remove.

Prototype workaround

  • Find the generated provider assigned to :coreclr.
  • Rewrite its class to a Java no-op provider after type-map generation.
  • Keep the primary NativeAOT provider and no-op provider through R8.
  • Verify packaged manifest and loaded DSOs, not just source declarations.

A product feature should let Java-only secondary processes opt out of automatic managed runtime initialization; manifest rewriting is prototype scaffolding.

What isolation costs

The application is now a small distributed system.

101.0 MiB

NativeAOT total PSS

Full dashboard after five editor handoffs.

134.5 MiB

CoreCLR total PSS

Warm runtime after closing five embedded windows.

Single emulator snapshot. Memory needs repeated physical-device measurement and a representative production graph.

  • IPC: auth, navigation arguments, readiness, and results cross a boundary.
  • Lifecycle: Android can reclaim or restart the heavy process independently.
  • UX: task stack, transitions, themes, accessibility, and deep links must remain coherent.
  • Operations: logs, traces, health, and crashes must correlate two PIDs.
  • Distribution: linking and normal ZIP compression reduce the dual-runtime APK to 31.55 MiB.

Current verdict

The runtime hypothesis works. The product boundary needs design.

Proven

  • One signed APK carries both complete runtime payloads.
  • NativeAOT renders login or persisted-auth dashboard first.
  • CoreCLR warms asynchronously in an isolated process.
  • The large MAUI assembly graph is callable and prepared.
  • Runtime DSOs, signals, heaps, and crash fate are separated.
  • Android can rebind and rebuild the heavy runtime process.

Next decisive experiments

  • Remove the input-service workaround by fixing typed service activation in the manually initialized host.
  • Define the IPC contract for auth, routes, state, cancellation, and errors.
  • Exercise lifecycle pressure across suspend, low memory, configuration, and upgrades.
  • Measure physical devices across cold install, warm caches, and representative app graphs.
  • Adapt warmup timing to device class, navigation intent, and memory pressure instead of using a fixed delay.

Progressive .NET for Android

Make the runtime a progressively loaded capability, not a prerequisite for useful pixels.

The two-process prototype preserves the startup advantage while replacing signal-handler coexistence with a stronger, testable isolation boundary.
